LegoSim: a first-person LEGO building simulator — technical report

An engine that executes building actions on LEGO models (connector-level physics rules, preconditions, refusal codes), a planner that turns official instructions into action lists, a mistake generator, and a renderer that turns each executed action into realistic head-camera video of a person building, with per-frame labels and controllable scenes.

3. How it works

9 modules, one direction of flow. Instructions (LDraw model file with steps) → planner (action list) → engine (executes each action: checks the precondition, applies the effect or refuses with a code) → motion layer (timed hand, part and head motion toward each state the engine produced) → renderer (Blender Cycles, a seated human, head-mounted camera) → video and labels. Mistakes are edits of the action list; scene factors are a settings record read by the renderer.

3.2 Connectors

Every part carries connector points from the LDCad snap library (studs, anti-stud tubes, pins, holes, axles, bars, clips): position, axis, gender, and a cross-section profile (e.g. round, radius 6, length 4 LDU). A male and a female connector are compatible if a radius class matches (an axle also fits a round hole). They engage when their axes are parallel (|cos| ≥ 0.995), at most 0.6 LDU apart sideways, and overlap along the axis; the overlap is the insertion depth, seat = min(1, depth / 4 LDU). A short connector (≤ 4 LDU, a stud) holds one partner; long ones (bars, pins) pass through several parts. Connectors can be addressed by index or by grid address (face, column, row) in stud pitches (20 LDU).

3.3 Placing a part: mate

The pose of the held part is solved in closed form from the two connector frames (origin = connector start, z = axis, x = a fixed perpendicular): a_m = flip·a_f, p_f = p_m − slide·a_m, x_m = R(a_m, turn)·x_f. Then, in order:

  1. a placement within 0.6 LDU / 0.1 of a planned part's official pose takes that exact pose (source models store slightly non-orthonormal rotations);
  2. a misalignment within 2.0 LDU / 6° self-aligns when pressed; a turn within 8° of the 90° stud grid snaps to it;
  3. if anything engages: refused if the part would interpenetrate another (COLLISION) or could not have been pushed in along the joint axis from a joint length + 1 LDU out (APPROACH_BLOCKED; stud-length joints, exempt at official poses); else mated, recording every engagement at that pose;
  4. if nothing engages: the part backs off along the joint to where the connector tips touch. If that direction is upward it rests there, at the lowest height where it touches nothing (on a neighbour's top if higher, up to 24 LDU); otherwise nothing holds it and it falls onto the table beside the model.

affordances(part) enumerates every (connector, target connector, turn, flip, slide) that engages without interpenetration (poses solved in one batch, duplicates merged).

3.5 From instructions to an action list

The model file is read step by step (sub-models expanded; part definitions embedded in the file become parts; a flexible hose drawn as segments becomes one part). Parts are taken in manual order. A part that touches nothing built so far starts a group, made on a free spot on the table (the first spot where its whole finished footprint clears everything else) — a sub-build. When a later part links two groups, the later one is attached to the earlier when its own run of parts ends; a sub-build still on the table at the end is attached to whatever it has a joint with (separate objects, like a figure beside a car, stay separate). Every placement becomes mate on the deepest real connector engagement with the part it goes onto, if that reproduces the official pose exactly; otherwise (no connector data) a pose-level place accepted through a trusted link. Optionally parts start in numbered bags (contiguous step runs, ~40 parts a bag) with open_bag inserted before each bag's first part. Replay checks the resulting structure against the official model.

3.6 Progress tracking

After any action every built part is compared with the official model, after aligning each separate model on one of its parts: correct, misplaced, not pressed down (right place, up to 4 LDU high), resting, foreign (not in the plan: wrong part or colour), loose. Complete = all correct, in as many pieces as the official model.

3.7 Mistakes and fixes

A mistake is a different choice of mate parameters at one placement, taken from what is feasible at that moment (affordances), so it is accepted wherever it is physically possible; a candidate that ends at the correct pose anyway (symmetric part, a gap that self-seats) is not a mistake. The rest of the build then runs through the engine unchanged, which decides what follows (continues, or a later step is refused). A fix is notice + taking the part off (fingers, or the separator where fingers can't grip; parts locking it in come off first and go back afterwards) + the correct action, after any delay. A recovery succeeds when the resulting build matches the expected construction state.

3.8 Motion

Each accepted action becomes phases (e.g. pick: reach 0.6 s, grasp 0.3 s; mate: lift 0.25, carry 0.5 + 1.2 s per metre, align 0.3, press 0.35, release 0.2, retract 0.5), eased, scaled by a per-action speed factor (log-normal, σ 0.18). Hand targets are corrected so the thumb–index pinch lands on the part. The head follows the gaze target through a critically damped second-order system (ω = 7.0 rad/s). Styled runs add, at set rates: misaligned landings that slide into place (35%), visual search (35% × loose parts / 20), re-grips when a part must be tipped over, missed grasps (4%), hesitation over look-alikes (25%), looking up at a new step (8%), glances at the booklet. The dominant hand reaches up to 30 cm across the midline; the idle hand then pulls back.

3.9 Hands and grasps

Each pick chooses a pinch grasp: an approach direction (3 elevations × 8 directions around the part) and how far the elbow is swung out (normal or wide), defined in the part's own frame so the hand turns with the part while carrying it. Feasible grasps come from above both where the part lies and where it goes, from a comfortable side for that hand. Since the builder knows where the part goes, the grasp is scored by how much of the part the head camera still sees at the moment it is pressed on: the scorer poses the same MakeHuman rig the renderer uses (arm inverse kinematics, posed skin and clothes) and ray-casts from the eye to 14 points on the part; the score adds a comfort term and a grasp is drawn among the best (softmax), so grasps vary. A part that must be tipped over on the way is turned in the fingers and re-gripped with a grasp chosen for where it goes. The free hand steadies the model from its own side at the spot that hides least, or stays back if every spot would hide more than 30 %. The finger direction, pinch point and elbow setting are recorded per frame, and the renderer's arm solver uses them.

10. Limits

  • A decorative or angled part can lack usable connector data and be placed at their exact official pose through a trusted link instead of a connector-level mate.
  • Collisions use oriented boxes, not exact shapes; no dynamics (parts don't bounce or roll; falling is a drop to the table).
  • Hands follow targets by inverse kinematics (no finger-contact simulation); one seated builder; LEGO only.
